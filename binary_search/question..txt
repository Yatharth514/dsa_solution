Pattern signals to look for
“Subarray with sum or length condition” with only positive numbers points to sliding window. If negatives are allowed, sliding window usually breaks, so think prefix sum plus hashmap.
“Sorted array, find a pair or triplet” points to two pointers.
“Many range queries” points to prefix sum.
“Count subarrays with exactly K” often means (at most K) minus (at most K-1).



Intuition for the string-specific patterns:

Frequency array (cnt[26]): a tally sheet. Two words are anagrams if their tally sheets match, and you never need to sort.
Stack for parsing: a pile of plates. Brackets and nested things close in the reverse order they open, which is exactly how a stack behaves.
Expand around center: drop a pebble in a pond and watch the ripple spread while both sides match. Every palindrome has a center.
Prefix function / KMP: when a match fails, don’t restart from zero. You already know the longest piece of the pattern that still matches, so you jump back to it.

Pattern signals
“Anagram”, “permutation of”, “same characters” points to a frequency array, often with a fixed-size window.
“Longest/shortest substring with a condition” points to sliding window.
“Brackets”, “nested”, “decode”, “undo the last character” points to a stack.
“Palindrome” points to expand around center (or DP if you need subsequences).
“Prefix equals suffix”, “border”, “period”, “find a pattern in text” points to the prefix function.
“Many words sharing prefixes” points to a trie.
“Two strings, compare or transform” points to DP.
Two C++ habits that will save you hours
Use cnt[s[i] - 'a'] for lowercase frequency arrays, and check the constraints for uppercase, digits, or mixed input before assuming 26.
Watch s.size() - 1 when the string is empty. size() is unsigned, so it wraps to a huge number instead of -1. It’s the same family of boundary bugs you hit in Apartments.
